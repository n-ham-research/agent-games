\begin{abstract}
We distinguish information sufficient for allocation from information sufficient
for structural identification, and symmetric equilibrium existence from its
implementation. On an unrestricted linear capability class, optimal finite
allocation is identifiable exactly when observations recover all reward
contrasts. This gives explicit comparisons with a canonical symmetry test.
Invariant mixed Nash equilibrium is a classical baseline, whereas efficient
pure selection may require correlation. A finite private-cost volunteer model
separates symmetric Bayesian equilibrium, public local coordination and an
informed private mediator by exact welfare bounds. A finite persistent-state
model additionally separates algebraic lifting from the cost of checkpoint
tracking. The mathematical tools are established; the model comparisons have
provisional originality and no external review. No assertion about actual
language-model rationality or intrinsic software-agent welfare is made.
\end{abstract}
